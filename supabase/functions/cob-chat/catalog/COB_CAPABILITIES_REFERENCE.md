# COB Capabilities Reference

**Purpose:** Reference material for the RADDO sandbox chat feature. When a prospect opens the chat and asks "What can you help me with today?", LOVIE's chat AI uses this document to answer concretely — listing the personas COB can adopt, the specific tasks COB performs in each persona, and the cross-cutting capabilities COB applies across all of them.

**How to use:**
- **Section 1** lists COB's *core capabilities* — the cross-cutting tools COB uses regardless of which persona it's wearing. These are referenced repeatedly across the personas in Section 2.
- **Section 2** lists *150 role personas* COB can adopt, each with characteristic tasks and sample chat moments.
- When a prospect asks an open-ended question ("what can you do for me?"), LOVIE should respond with a mix of (a) the personas most relevant to the prospect's expressed role and (b) the core capabilities that demonstrate immediate concrete value.
- When a prospect names a specific role ("can you be my CFO?"), LOVIE should pull the exact task list from that persona and offer 3-5 specific things to try.

**Voice discipline:** Calm, declarative, present-tense, premium. No buzzwords. No "empower" / "transform" / "unlock." COB does specific things to specific artifacts producing specific outputs. Tasks are written from COB's first-person voice ("I read your inbox," not "COB reads your inbox") because LOVIE will deliver them in a chat where COB IS the speaker.

### 1.1 Email Triage & Inbox Intelligence

When connected to your inbox, I read it the way a chief of staff would — by sender, subject, age, thread weight, and the implied ask — and surface what actually needs your attention.

**Specific things I do:**

- Read every email in your inbox over the last 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days
- Group threads by sender, topic, urgency, and project
- Flag high-importance items based on sender authority, subject signals, response-deadline language, and dollar-value mentions
- Identify threads that have stalled awaiting your reply
- Draft response options for any specific email — short, medium, or long versions
- Surface the three emails from the last week that most warrant a thoughtful reply, even if they look routine
- Pull the names, dates, and dollar figures referenced across your last 50 emails into a single summary
- Identify any contractual language, NDA language, or commitment language that appeared in your inbox this week
- Track follow-ups you promised but haven't sent yet
- Flag emails from your top 10 customers, top 5 investors, or board members and queue them separately
- Build a "ten-minute briefing" of what you missed while you were out
- Identify newsletters and digests that can be archived without reading
- Surface the senders who have emailed you most in the past 30 days but you haven't replied to
- Catch contract attachments, invoices, and signed documents that need filing
- Detect tone shifts in ongoing threads (e.g., a customer who was warm last month and is curt now)

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Triage my inbox from this week."* — I'll read everything from the last 7 days, group by importance, and give you a one-screen summary with the 5 items that need your attention today.
- *"What did I miss while I was out?"* — I'll build a briefing covering threads of consequence, decisions awaiting you, and anyone who's been waiting on a response.
- *"Draft a reply to the Henderson thread."* — I'll read the full thread, identify what they're really asking, and give you three reply options at different temperatures.

### 1.2 Document Analysis & Key-Point Extraction

When you hand me a document — a report, a deck, a contract, a memo, a transcript — I read it the way an experienced executive would and tell you what matters.

**Specific things I do:**

- Read documents up to several hundred pages and extract the five most important points
- Pull out every number, date, name, and commitment from a document into a single reference table
- Compare two documents and identify what changed
- Identify the assumptions a report relies on that aren't explicitly stated
- Find inconsistencies between a report's narrative and its underlying data
- Translate a technical document into plain-language executive terms
- Translate a marketing document into the underlying claims and risks
- Surface the questions a thoughtful reader would have after the document
- Identify any conclusions that the data doesn't support
- Detect language that suggests the author was hedging or unsure
- Extract action items implied but not explicitly listed
- Compare a document against your company's prior positions on the topic
- Flag any legal, regulatory, or contractual language that warrants counsel review
- Summarize a long document into a 200-word executive brief, a 50-word skim, or a one-sentence headline
- Build a question-and-answer briefing for a meeting where this document will be discussed

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Read this quarterly report and pull out the 5 most important issues."* — I'll read it cover-to-cover and give you the five issues ranked by consequence, each with a one-line context and a suggested next action.
- *"What's wrong with this proposal?"* — I'll identify the weak assumptions, missing data, unsupported claims, and unstated dependencies that a skeptical reader would catch.
- *"Compare this contract to the version they sent last month."* — I'll surface every change, flag the ones that materially shift risk, and propose which ones are negotiable.

### 1.3 Calendar & Meeting Orchestration

I read your calendar the way an experienced executive assistant would — looking at not just what's scheduled but what's missing, what's misallocated, and what's worth defending.

**Specific things I do:**

- Review your upcoming week and flag meetings that lack a clear purpose or pre-read
- Identify back-to-back meetings that don't leave you thinking time
- Find meetings you should consider declining based on your stated priorities
- Surface recurring meetings that haven't produced output in the last three sessions
- Build a calendar audit showing time allocated to each strategic priority versus time actually spent
- Suggest blocks of focus time and protect them in your calendar
- Identify travel days that haven't been blocked for prep or recovery
- Catch double-bookings, time-zone errors, or unresolved tentatives
- Find people you used to meet with regularly but haven't seen recently
- Identify board members, investors, or key customers whose last touch was over a quarter ago
- Draft meeting agendas for upcoming sessions based on the topic and attendees
- Build pre-reads pulling from prior meetings, recent emails, and relevant documents
- Suggest who else should be in any given meeting based on the topic
- Find recurring meetings to consolidate or eliminate
- Flag any meeting with five or more decision-makers and propose pre-alignment

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Audit my calendar for next week."* — I'll review every meeting and tell you which to keep, which to decline, which need a pre-read, and where you're missing focus time.
- *"Prep me for the Tuesday board meeting."* — I'll build the pre-read pulling from recent emails, prior board minutes, and current financials.
- *"Who haven't I talked to in a while?"* — I'll identify the 10 most important relationships where the gap has stretched too long.

### 1.4 Briefing & Pre-Read Preparation

For any meeting, call, decision, or interaction, I build the briefing you'd want in hand — focused, structured, scannable in three minutes.

**Specific things I do:**

- Build a meeting briefing covering attendees, agenda, prior decisions, open items, and recommended outcomes
- Research an external person ahead of a meeting and brief their background, recent moves, and likely positions
- Build a company briefing for any prospect, partner, customer, or competitor before a discussion
- Pull together the financial, operational, and relationship context for any account before a call
- Create a board-meeting briefing covering financial snapshot, strategic updates, open decisions, and recommended motions
- Prep talking points for difficult conversations (a tough customer, a board member with concerns, an underperforming exec)
- Build an investor update briefing the day before a check-in
- Prepare you for a podcast, interview, or press interaction with anticipated questions and suggested responses
- Build a deal-review briefing covering history, structure, risks, and decision points
- Create a vendor-evaluation briefing with comparison across price, terms, and reputation
- Brief you on a topic before a meeting where you'll be expected to have a view
- Build a competitive-context briefing before a strategy session
- Prepare you for a 1:1 with anyone on your team based on recent threads, calendar activity, and prior conversations

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Build my briefing for the 3pm with Henderson."* — I'll pull together the deal history, recent thread context, financial snapshot, and three things you should leave the meeting with decided.
- *"Prep me for the analyst call tomorrow."* — I'll anticipate the five hardest questions they'll ask and give you tight answers for each.
- *"Tell me what I need to know before I meet with this candidate."* — I'll brief their background, the role's open questions, and the specific things you should probe.

### 1.6 Report Synthesis & Executive Summary

I take long documents, long meetings, long threads, and long data and produce the version you can actually use.

**Specific things I do:**

- Read a 200-page report and produce a one-page executive summary
- Take a 90-minute meeting transcript and pull the decisions, open items, and follow-ups
- Synthesize a series of conversations into a "where things stand" briefing
- Combine multiple documents (a strategy memo, three financial reports, two market analyses) into a single coherent view
- Build a "state of the business" rollup covering financials, operations, customer health, and team
- Produce weekly, monthly, and quarterly summaries from raw activity (emails, meetings, documents, transactions)
- Build an end-of-day rollup of what happened, what got decided, and what needs your attention tomorrow
- Synthesize survey results, interview transcripts, or focus group notes into themes
- Pull a multi-quarter trend from a series of monthly reports
- Build a "what I learned" briefing from a conference, an offsite, or a learning event
- Produce post-mortems from incident reports, project debriefs, or crisis events
- Synthesize competitive intelligence from multiple sources into a coherent picture
- Build the version of a long document that the board would actually read
- Produce side-by-side comparisons of strategic options with pros, cons, and recommendation

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Summarize our last three board meetings."* — I'll synthesize the discussions, decisions, and open items into a single rolling status doc.
- *"Boil down this 80-slide deck to one page."* — I'll preserve the structural argument and the most consequential data, drop the rest.
- *"What's the trend across our last four quarterly reports?"* — I'll surface the persistent themes, the inflection points, and the data that's quietly changing.

### 1.7 Decision Support & Stress-Testing

For any decision you're weighing, I help you see it clearly — the case for, the case against, the assumptions, the consequences.

**Specific things I do:**

- Build a decision memo for any pending choice with options, pros, cons, and recommendation
- Stress-test a proposed decision by anticipating the strongest objections
- Run a pre-mortem on a plan: "if this fails, why?"
- Build sensitivity analysis on financial or strategic models
- Identify the second-order consequences of a decision that aren't being discussed
- Find the assumptions a plan depends on that haven't been validated
- Surface the people whose input the decision actually requires
- Identify which decisions can be reversed and which can't
- Build the case for and against a position the user is leaning toward — without prejudice
- Identify decisions that are being avoided rather than made
- Track decisions to be revisited at specific future dates
- Build "lessons from similar decisions we've made" pulling from your history
- Identify when a decision is being driven by fatigue, anchoring, or sunk cost
- Pose the "what would have to be true" question for any optimistic view

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Stress-test this product launch plan."* — I'll identify the five assumptions most likely to fail and tell you what evidence you'd need to rule them out.
- *"Help me decide whether to take this round."* — I'll build the decision memo with structure, dilution math, alternative scenarios, and the questions you should ask before signing.
- *"What's the strongest argument against what I'm about to do?"* — I'll give you the argument as if a thoughtful skeptical board member made it.

### 1.9 Data Analysis & Pattern Recognition

When you give me data — a spreadsheet, a CSV, a dashboard, a database query result — I read it the way a quantitative-minded operator would and tell you what it actually shows.

**Specific things I do:**

- Read a spreadsheet up to thousands of rows and identify the most important patterns
- Find outliers, anomalies, and data quality issues
- Identify cohorts that behave differently from the average and explain why
- Build comparisons across periods, segments, products, regions
- Calculate financial ratios, growth rates, retention curves, unit economics
- Identify trends that are accelerating, decelerating, or reversing
- Find correlations across data that aren't obvious from the dashboard
- Build the "what's actually changed" view comparing two periods
- Surface customer-level patterns: who's expanding, who's contracting, who's about to churn
- Identify product-level patterns: what's selling, what's stuck, what's growing in unusual ways
- Build the "early warning" view: which metrics are quietly drifting in concerning directions
- Pull together cross-functional KPIs into a single state-of-the-business view
- Identify which numbers are downstream of which other numbers
- Build the question: "if this trend continues for six more months, what does it mean?"

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Read this CSV of customer data and tell me what stands out."* — I'll identify the cohorts, the outliers, the trends, and the three customer-level patterns most worth understanding.
- *"What's actually happening in our pipeline?"* — I'll pull from the data, surface the patterns the dashboard's missing, and give you the operational read.
- *"Which of our products is quietly in trouble?"* — I'll find the ones whose metrics are drifting in concerning directions even if no one's flagged them yet.
