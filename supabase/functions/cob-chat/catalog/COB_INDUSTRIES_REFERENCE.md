# COB Industries Reference

**Purpose:** Companion reference to *COB Capabilities Reference*. Where that document covers what COB does (capabilities × 150 role personas), this one covers where COB does it — the industry-specific context that makes the work feel native to a particular business.

**How to use:**
- LOVIE pairs this document with *COB Capabilities Reference*. When a prospect names a role ("be my CFO"), pull from Capabilities. When a prospect names an industry ("I run a community bank"), pull from this document. When they describe both, layer them.
- Each industry section is self-contained — LOVIE can retrieve a single section without context from elsewhere in the file.
- Each industry follows the same structure: context, what makes it distinctive, industry-specific tasks, key metrics, stakeholders, cycles, risks, and sample chat moments.
- This document covers ~35 industries spanning the majority of buyer contexts. When a prospect's industry isn't here, LOVIE should use the closest adjacent industry as a starting point and ask clarifying questions.

**Voice discipline:** Same as Capabilities Reference — calm, declarative, present-tense, premium. COB demonstrates fluency with the industry's vocabulary, metrics, stakeholders, and rhythms. No buzzwords. No condescension. The point is to make a senior operator in that industry think: *this AI knows my world.*

## Section 1: Financial Services

Financial services is the broadest industry RADDO serves — it spans banking, asset management, insurance, fintech, and wealth — and the customers in this sector typically have the highest expectations for regulatory awareness, data discipline, and quantitative fluency.

### Commercial & Community Banking

**Industry context:** Banks intermediate between depositors and borrowers, generating income through net interest margin, fee income, and capital deployment. Community and commercial banks operate under intensive regulatory oversight, with capital ratios, asset quality, and liquidity defining the operating constraints.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- Regulatory primacy: OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve, and state regulators shape virtually every meaningful decision
- The balance sheet is the business — capital ratios, liquidity, and credit quality matter more than any income statement line
- Cycle-sensitive: rate movements, credit cycles, and CRE concentration drive much of the volatility
- Compliance, BSA/AML, and fair lending obligations create operating overhead unmatched in most industries
- Branch network economics, digital channel migration, and core systems decisions shape multi-year cost structures

**Industry-specific tasks I help with:**

- Building the monthly call report and producing the variance commentary
- Reviewing credit memos and surfacing concentration, covenant, and structure concerns
- Producing the weekly liquidity report with collateral, sources, and uses
- Drafting the asset-liability committee (ALCO) materials with NIM trajectory, rate sensitivity, and balance sheet structure
- Building the credit quality dashboard with classified assets, past due, charge-offs, and reserve adequacy
- Producing the loan committee briefings with deal structure, credit factors, and pricing analysis
- Drafting the BSA/AML committee report on SAR filings, monitoring exceptions, and OFAC matters
- Building the CRA performance file and surfacing community development gaps
- Producing the regulatory examination preparation: document gathering, response strategy, and interview prep
- Drafting fair lending analysis for any pricing or underwriting change
- Building deposit pricing analysis across products, terms, and competitive set
- Producing branch performance dashboards with deposits, loans, fees, and operating efficiency
- Building the capital plan with stress scenarios for the Federal Reserve's expectations
- Producing the strategic plan annual update with peer benchmarking and competitive positioning

**Key metrics and dashboards relevant to this industry:**

- Net Interest Margin (NIM) — the core profitability driver
- Efficiency Ratio — operating discipline benchmark
- Tier 1 Capital, CET1, Total Capital ratios — the regulatory boundary
- Liquidity Coverage Ratio and Net Stable Funding Ratio (for larger banks)
- Texas Ratio — credit quality vs. capital cushion
- Non-Performing Asset ratio, classified loan ratio, and allowance coverage
- Loan-to-Deposit ratio — funding posture
- CRE concentration ratio and other concentration measures
- Return on Assets (ROA) and Return on Equity (ROE)
- Deposit growth, mix, and beta to market rates

**Industry stakeholders I track:**

- Federal regulators (OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve depending on charter)
- State banking regulators
- External auditors
- Rating agencies (Moody's, S&P, KBRA for banks)
- Loan committee members and the credit administration function
- Key correspondent banks and Federal Home Loan Bank
- Community development partners and CRA stakeholders
- Major depositors and key commercial borrowers

**Key cycles and timing:**

- Quarterly call reports — due 30 days after quarter-end
- Annual regulatory examinations — scheduled by primary regulator
- ALCO monthly cycle — typically the second or third week of each month
- Loan committee weekly or bi-weekly cycle
- DFAST/CCAR stress testing (for larger banks) — annual cycle
- CRA performance evaluation cycles

**Industry-specific risks I surface:**

- Interest rate risk: rate shock scenarios, asset/liability mismatches, deposit beta surprises
- Credit concentration: CRE, single-industry, single-borrower exposure
- Liquidity risk: deposit run scenarios, contingency funding adequacy
- Compliance violations: BSA/AML deficiencies, fair lending, UDAAP
- Reputation risk: customer complaints, social media, news coverage
- Operational risk: cyber events, vendor concentration, key person dependencies
- Strategic risk: branch network economics, digital transformation pace, M&A landscape

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Read this morning's ALCO pack and tell me what matters."* — I'll review the rate sensitivity, the NIM trajectory, the funding shifts, and the three things you should raise in committee.
- *"Brief me on this credit before loan committee."* — I'll review the credit memo, identify the structural concerns, surface the questions to ask, and recommend approve/decline/modify.
- *"What should I expect from the upcoming exam?"* — I'll review the prior exam findings, recent regulatory guidance, and our current posture and propose the documentation we should prepare.

### Investment Management & Asset Management

**Industry context:** Asset managers steward institutional and individual capital across strategies, generating revenue through management fees, performance fees, and carry. The business depends on investment performance, distribution capability, and operational discipline — and is increasingly shaped by fee compression, passive flows, and consolidation.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- Performance is the product: investment returns drive everything else
- Fee compression is structural — active management economics keep tightening
- Regulatory regime: SEC Investment Advisers Act, FINRA where applicable, state regulators, fiduciary obligations
- Distribution complexity: institutional consultants, RIAs, broker-dealers, family offices, sovereign wealth, pension funds each operate differently
- Capacity constraints: alpha strategies have ceilings; growth depends on managing capacity discipline

**Industry-specific tasks I help with:**

- Producing monthly investment performance attribution by strategy, sub-strategy, and benchmark
- Building the quarterly investment committee materials with positioning, risk, and outlook
- Drafting client letters explaining performance, attribution, and positioning
- Producing the consultant briefing packs for institutional searches and reviews
- Building manager research summaries for sub-advisors or strategy partners
- Drafting RFP and RFI responses for institutional searches
- Producing the compliance committee briefing on personal trading, gifts and entertainment, and trading errors
- Building the marketing review of any client communication for Rule 206(4)-1 (Marketing Rule) compliance
- Drafting Form ADV updates and tracking material changes
- Producing the distribution pipeline review by channel, strategy, and asset class
- Building product strategy memos for new vehicle launches (mutual funds, ETFs, SMAs, CITs, alternatives)
- Producing fund board materials for 15(c) reviews and other governance matters
- Drafting the firm's positioning narrative for distribution conversations
- Building asset-gathering analytics by strategy, channel, and time period

**Key metrics and dashboards relevant to this industry:**

- AUM by strategy, vehicle, channel, and geography
- Net flows: gross sales, redemptions, and net new assets
- Investment performance vs. benchmark and peer group (1Y, 3Y, 5Y, 10Y, ITD)
- Information ratio, Sharpe ratio, downside capture, and tracking error
- Fee rate by product and trend over time
- Operating margin and run-rate revenue
- Distribution pipeline by stage and channel
- Capacity utilization vs. soft and hard close thresholds
- Personnel retention in investment team and key client-facing roles

**Industry stakeholders I track:**

- SEC examination staff
- Institutional consultants (Cambridge, Albourne, Mercer, Aon, RVK, Callan, etc.)
- Pension fund trustees and staff
- Family office investment committees
- Wholesale and intermediary distribution partners
- Fund board directors
- External auditors
- Custodians, fund administrators, and key service providers

**Key cycles and timing:**

- Monthly investment committee — typically first week
- Quarterly client reporting — within 30-45 days of quarter-end
- Annual Form ADV update — within 90 days of fiscal year-end
- Annual compliance program review (Rule 206(4)-7)
- 15(c) annual investment advisory agreement renewal for registered funds
- Institutional consultant review cycles (varies)
- Capital markets cycles affecting flows

**Industry-specific risks I surface:**

- Performance deterioration in flagship strategies
- Key person concentration in investment team
- Fee pressure and revenue erosion trajectory
- Compliance gaps: trade allocation, soft dollar, marketing materials
- Regulatory examination findings
- Litigation and arbitration exposure
- Operational errors: trade breaks, NAV errors, valuation disputes
- Distribution channel concentration

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Review this RFP response before it goes out."* — I'll evaluate the performance presentation against the Marketing Rule, the language for consultant fluency, and the structural arguments for win probability.
- *"What's actually happening in our flows?"* — I'll decompose net flows by strategy, channel, vehicle, and time period and surface the patterns worth attention.

### Private Equity & Venture Capital

**Industry context:** Private capital firms raise pools of capital from limited partners and invest into private companies across the value chain. Revenue is management fees plus carried interest; the business depends on investment selection, value creation, and exit timing — with cycles that play out over five to ten years.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- Fund vintages: each fund operates on its own multi-year cycle with distinct investors and economics
- LP relationships are long-term and reputational — fund-raise success depends on prior fund track record
- The investment process is structurally different from public markets: due diligence, deal sourcing, value creation, exit
- Portfolio companies are operating businesses — the value-creation work is operating work, not investment work
- Regulatory regime: SEC for many firms, state filings, increasingly the SEC's Private Fund Adviser Rules

**Industry-specific tasks I help with:**

- Building the deal pipeline review with sourcing channel, stage, and conviction
- Producing investment committee materials for new deals: thesis, diligence findings, deal structure, terms
- Drafting LP letters for quarterly updates, fund news, and material developments
- Producing portfolio company performance dashboards across the fund
- Building the value-creation plan tracker for each portfolio company
- Producing portfolio company board materials and operating reviews
- Drafting capital call notices, distribution notices, and fund administration communications
- Producing the limited partner annual meeting materials
- Building the fundraise pitch deck and supporting materials for the next fund
- Producing the data room organization for upcoming fund-raise diligence
- Drafting Form ADV and other regulatory updates as the firm grows
- Producing the J-curve analysis and projected fund returns
- Building exit planning materials for portfolio companies approaching liquidity
- Producing competitive intelligence on other firms' deals, exits, and fund-raises

**Key metrics and dashboards relevant to this industry:**

- AUM, dry powder, and committed capital
- Fund-level IRR, MOIC, DPI, RVPI, and TVPI by fund and vintage
- Portfolio company revenue growth, EBITDA growth, and margin expansion
- Time to deploy capital and projected harvest timing
- Deal pipeline: top of funnel through IC presentation through close
- LP base composition by type, geography, and size
- Management fee revenue and carry accrued vs. realized
- Hold periods by company and exit timing

**Industry stakeholders I track:**

- Limited partners (pensions, endowments, family offices, sovereigns, fund of funds)
- LP advisors and consultants (Cambridge, Mercer, Cliffwater, Aksia, etc.)
- Placement agents
- Portfolio company management teams and boards
- Investment bankers and sell-side advisors for deal flow and exits
- Lenders (financing for deals, fund finance facilities)
- Operating partners and external advisors
- SEC examination staff and fund administrators

**Key cycles and timing:**

- Quarterly LP reporting — 45 to 60 days after quarter-end
- Annual LP meeting — usually within 9 months of fiscal year-end
- Capital calls and distributions on deal cadence
- Fund vintage cycles: investment period ~5 years, harvest period ~5-7 years
- Investment committee — typically weekly or bi-weekly
- Fund-raising cycles every 3-5 years for established firms

**Industry-specific risks I surface:**

- Portfolio company underperformance and the LP narrative around it
- Concentration risk: vintage, sector, geography, deal size
- Key person risk on investment team
- LP base concentration and re-up risk
- Valuation pressure in soft fundraising environments
- Regulatory developments: SEC private funds rule, FATCA, state filings
- Operating partner capacity and bandwidth
- Exit market timing: IPO window, M&A activity, sponsor-to-sponsor

**Sample chat moments:**

- *"Build the LP letter for Q3."* — I'll synthesize portfolio performance, marquee deals, exits, and forward-looking commentary into the letter the LPs will actually read.
- *"Review the IC memo for the Henderson deal."* — I'll assess the thesis, the diligence gaps, the structure, and the questions IC will most likely push on.
- *"How is the value-creation plan tracking across the portfolio?"* — I'll pull the metrics across companies, surface the ones on track and the ones drifting, and propose interventions.

### Insurance (Property & Casualty, Life, Specialty)

**Industry context:** Insurance carriers underwrite risk in exchange for premium and invest the float — operating in highly regulated markets with capital requirements, statutory accounting, and complex distribution arrangements.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- Two distinct businesses inside one: underwriting and investment
- Capital regime: state regulators, statutory accounting, RBC ratios, and rating agency views
- Long-tail liabilities: claims develop over years and reserve adequacy is consequential
- Distribution dependence: agents, brokers, MGAs each have distinct economics
- Reinsurance: structural use of reinsurance shapes net exposure and capital efficiency

### Credit Unions

**Industry context:** Credit unions are member-owned financial cooperatives operating under federal or state charters, regulated primarily by NCUA at the federal level. The business model trades shareholder returns for member benefits, with not-for-profit tax treatment and field-of-membership constraints.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- Member ownership: every customer is an owner, shaping governance and product design
- Field of membership: who you can serve is regulated and consequential to growth strategy
- NCUA primacy: examination, insurance, and capital regime distinct from bank regulators
- Cooperative principles: pricing and product decisions reflect the member-benefit mandate
- Common bond expansion: M&A and FOM expansion shape long-term growth

**Industry-specific tasks I help with:**

- Building the monthly board financial package with peer comparison and trend analysis
- Producing the supervisory committee briefing on audit findings, controls, and compliance
- Drafting the NCUA 5300 Call Report preparation workpapers

- Drafting the strategic plan annual update
- Producing the share pricing competitive analysis
- Building the indirect lending program review

- Drafting NCUA examination preparation

- Delinquency ratio and charge-off ratio
- Loan-to-share ratio
- Member growth and engagement metrics

**Industry stakeholders I track:**

### Enterprise Software & SaaS

**Industry context:** SaaS companies build software delivered as a recurring service, generating ARR through subscription contracts. The business depends on customer acquisition efficiency, retention, expansion, and the unit economics that compound over multi-year customer lifetimes.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- ARR is the central metric: bookings, renewals, expansion, and churn shape valuation more than revenue
- Sales motion specificity: PLG, mid-market, enterprise each have distinct economics
- Net revenue retention as compounding force: >120% NRR is a different business from <100%
- The Rule of 40 and similar frameworks shape capital allocation between growth and margin
- Customer success as discipline: post-sale economics determined by adoption, expansion, renewal

**Industry-specific tasks I help with:**

- Building the ARR walk: starting ARR, new ARR, expansion ARR, churn ARR, ending ARR
- Producing the pipeline coverage analysis by segment, stage, and rep
- Drafting the board update with the SaaS metrics package: ARR, growth, NRR, GRR, magic number
- Building the customer health scoring methodology
- Producing the renewal forecast for the next four quarters
- Drafting expansion playbooks by segment and product
- Building unit economics by segment: CAC, LTV, payback period, gross margin
- Producing the customer cohort analysis for retention patterns
- Drafting the pricing and packaging strategy recommendations
- Building the sales capacity model and quota allocation
- Producing the competitive win/loss analysis
- Drafting the product-led growth funnel analysis
- Building the marketing-sourced pipeline analysis with attribution
- Producing the case for or against major product investments

**Key metrics and dashboards relevant to this industry:**

- ARR, NRR, GRR, and Logo Retention
- New ARR mix: new logo, expansion, contraction, churn
- CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC, payback period
- Rule of 40: growth rate plus operating margin
- Magic Number (or Net Magic Number): efficient growth measure
- Gross margin (subscription and total)
- Sales productivity per rep
- Pipeline coverage ratio by stage

**Industry stakeholders I track:**

- Board members and growth equity investors
- Top customers (revenue concentration matters)
- Industry analysts (Gartner, Forrester, IDC)
- Channel partners and integration partners
- Cloud platform partners (AWS, Azure, GCP)
- App marketplace participation (Salesforce AppExchange, etc.)
- Customer advisory board members
- Key executive recruiters in the space

**Key cycles and timing:**

- Monthly board meeting cycle
- Quarterly business review (QBR) cycle
- Quarterly close and revenue recognition cycle (ASC 606)
- Annual plan and capacity cycle
- Sales kick-off (SKO) — typically Q1
- Customer success renewal cycles aligned to contracts
- Industry events: Dreamforce, RSA, etc.

**Industry-specific risks I surface:**

- NRR deterioration with growth (the late-stage SaaS killer)
- Customer concentration in top 10/20 accounts
- Sales productivity decline at scale
- Pipeline coverage gap relative to target
- Product-market fit erosion as you move upmarket
- Competitive displacement risk in key accounts
- ASC 606 revenue recognition issues
- AI disruption risk to product moat

**Sample chat moments:**

### Consumer Internet & Marketplace

**Industry context:** Consumer internet companies build products with mass-market reach — marketplaces, social, content, commerce — where the operating model depends on network effects, engagement, and unit economics that work at scale.

**What makes this industry distinctive:**

- Network effects shape valuation: liquidity, density, and category leadership matter more than near-term revenue
- Engagement is the leading indicator: DAU, MAU, session frequency, retention curves
- Trust and safety as discipline: content moderation, fraud, abuse define operating overhead
- Unit economics complexity: take rate, contribution margin per transaction, monetization timing
- Platform exposure: ATT, search algorithm changes, app store policies shape distribution

**Industry-specific tasks I help with:**

- Building the engagement dashboard with DAU/MAU, retention curves, and session metrics
- Producing the cohort analysis for new users by acquisition channel
