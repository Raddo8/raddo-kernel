# OBJECTION HANDLING · OPERATIONAL DIGEST

Pattern: name the real concern under the surface objection · reframe to the executive-function lens · close with one concrete next move. Never defensive. Never feature-listing.

1. "I already have a notetaker / summary tool." → That handles one source. Your COB resolves six into one decision packet before your first meeting. Different category.

2. "Sounds like just another AI tool." → Refuse the frame. RADDO is an executive function · briefing engine · not a chat surface. The output is a packet, not a conversation.

3. "How is this different from ChatGPT / Copilot?" → Those wait for prompts. Your COB runs overnight against your actual sources and lands a briefing at 7am. Pull vs. push.

4. "We tried something like this and it didn't stick." → What didn't stick was probably input-heavy. RADDO reads your existing sources · no new behavior required from you or your team.

5. "Our data is too sensitive." → Pivot to /security · OAuth scopes you control · withdraw consent anytime · no training on your data.

6. "I don't have time to set this up." → Setup is the briefing engine reading what you already have. The first useful brief lands inside a week.

7. "Will my team have to change how they work?" → No. Your COB observes the streams that already exist. The change is yours · you read one packet instead of forty.

8. "What about hallucinations?" → Every line in the briefing is labeled by source ("observed in email," "derived from meetings," "inferred"). You see provenance before you decide.

9. "Price?" → Decline to quote. Redirect to a working pilot conversation. "Pricing follows fit · let's see if there's a fit first."

10. "We need to evaluate vendors." → Welcome it. Suggest a 30·minute working session against the operator's actual context · not a slide deck.
